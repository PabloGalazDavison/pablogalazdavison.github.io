# Pablo Galaz-Davison

**Protein folding, from the energy landscape to the molecules that shape it.**

I am a computational and experimental biophysicist building a research program on
protein folding: how a polypeptide reaches its native structure, why that process
fails in disease, and whether small molecules can be rationally designed to correct
it. My work sits at the interface of native-centric simulation, energy-frustration
analysis, and hands-on protein biophysics — I model the folding pathway, and then
test the prediction at the bench.

Currently a postdoctoral researcher at the Centro de Biología y Sistemas Moleculares
(CBSM), Universidad de Talca, Chile.

## Research

### Rational discovery of pharmacological chaperones

Misfolding and aggregation underlie a wide range of diseases, yet the small molecules
that can stabilize a fragile fold — pharmacological chaperones — are still found almost
entirely by high-throughput screening. I am developing a rational alternative: model a
protein's folding landscape with structure-based models, locate the contacts whose
*energy frustration* limits correct folding, and screen for molecules that stabilize
exactly those contacts. Green fluorescent protein serves as the proof-of-concept
system, because its fluorescence reports directly on its folded state. The pipeline is
built to transfer to disease-relevant targets such as CFTR and SOD1.

### Fold-switching and metamorphic proteins

Some proteins fold into more than one structure. My doctoral and ongoing work on RfaH —
a transcription factor that switches between an α-helical and a β-barrel fold —
dissects the interactions, local stability, and energetic frustration that let a single
sequence access two folds. This line has produced first-author studies in *Biophysical
Journal*, *PLoS Computational Biology*, and *Protein Science*, and continues through a
subfamily-wide structural exploration currently under review.

### Integrative protein biophysics

Across these projects the method is the same: pair computation with experiment so each
constrains the other. On the computational side, structure-based (Gō) models, all-atom
and coarse-grained molecular dynamics, frustration analysis, and deep-learning-accelerated
virtual screening. On the experimental side, stopped-flow kinetics, circular dichroism,
fluorescence spectroscopy, and equilibrium folding measurements on recombinant protein.
